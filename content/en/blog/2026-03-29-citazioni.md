Yesterday I looked at my personal quotes page because I would like to integrate those quotes on this site (some at least).

This is one of my favorite quotes:

 > I'm not a great programmer,  
 > I'm a good programmer  
 > with great habits

[Kent Beck](https://www.kentbeck.com/)


{{< card >}}

*Result of AI Overview*

This mantra, popularized by Kent Beck, suggests that consistent, disciplined habits—such as refactoring, testing, and continuous learning—are more valuable than raw, innate talent. Being a "good programmer with great habits" emphasizes sustainability, quality, and maintainability over short-term heroics or complex, unreadable code.

- [Programming quotes that blew my mind](https://www.jorgemanrubia.com/2019/09/19/programming-quotes-that-blew-my-mind/)

Key Habits of Top Programmers:
- Constant Feedback Loops: Focus on shortening the time between writing code and validating it (e.g., TDD, immediate testing).
- Refactoring & Clean Code: Prioritize writing readable, maintainable code rather than just making it work.
- Continuous Learning: Driven by curiosity to learn new technologies and improve, rather than just fulfilling requirements.
- Strategic Laziness: Using tools, libraries, and automation effectively to save time.
- Collaboration & Communication: Emphasizing teamwork, mentorship, and effective communication over solitary, genius-level coding. 

This approach transforms programming from a high-pressure "genius" role into a sustainable, professional craft focused on long-term, high-quality results.

And this is another one that I wrote down on this date (Jul 13, 2023):

> Do what you can, with what you have, where you are.

**Theodore Roosevelt**

{{< card >}}

*Result of AI Overview*

“Do what you can, with what you have, where you are” is a famous aphorism attributed to the 26th president of the United States, Theodore Roosevelt. This quote, often referring to his friend, Squire Bill, is an invitation to practicality, resilience and immediate action, overcoming excuses related to lack of means or difficult situations.

**Meaning and Key Points:**
- **Immediate Action:** Don't wait for perfect conditions or ideal resources; start with what's available now.
- **Resilience:** Encourages not to be discouraged, to look at current resources with new eyes and to face challenges with serenity.
- **Autonomy:** Emphasizes the importance of doing one's best ("doing one's best") without expecting others to do what they cannot.
- **Resource in Limits:** The aphorism is considered a profound teaching, applicable to anyone in any circumstance, which transforms limits into opportunities.

In summary, it is a philosophy of life that focuses on individual change to achieve great results, cultivating self-confidence despite difficulties.
